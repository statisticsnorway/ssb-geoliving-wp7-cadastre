## Background: 
Cadastral and land parcel identification in Europe has a long history, playing a crucial role in land management, taxation, and statistical analysis. 
Historically, cadastral systems date back to the Roman Empire and the Napoleonic cadastre in the early 19th century, which systematically recorded land ownership and boundaries. 
Over time, cadastral maps became essential for legal property rights, urban planning, and infrastructure development.
Today, cadastral and land parcel data are widely used in various statistical domains. In agriculture, they help monitor land use, subsidies, and environmental impact. 
In real estate and taxation, cadastral records provide transparency in property valuation and transactions. They are also critical for regional planning, disaster risk management, and environmental protection, ensuring efficient land use and sustainable development.
With the digitalization of land records and the development of geospatial databases like the INSPIRE directive in the EU, cadastral data are increasingly integrated with other datasets to support policymaking and socio-economic analysis.
Objectives: This work package aims to develop and test methodologies for integrating cadastral and land parcel data with statistical production processes, improving spatial resolution, data interoperability, and relevance for key policy areas such as land use, housing, agriculture, taxation, and environmental monitoring. The work builds upon Norway’s well-established cadastral infrastructure and contributes to the broader goals of the Global Statistical Geospatial Framework (GSGF), the EU High-Value Datasets (HVD) directive, and the UN Integrated Geospatial Information Framework (IGIF).

## Rationale and Policy Relevance:
Cadastral and land parcel information is essential for delivering accurate, localized statistics relevant to land ownership, land use, building stock, property taxation, and spatial development. Integrating this information into statistical workflows can support:
•	The High Value Dataset Directive (cadastral parcels and buildings),
•	The INSPIRE Directive (themes: Cadastral Parcels, Land Use, Buildings),
•	EU initiatives on land monitoring, smart taxation, housing, and climate adaptation,
•	Reporting on SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) and SDG 15 (Life on Land).

## Scope and Activities
This WP will focus on using cadastral data as a linking layer and analytic base for enhanced statistics. Activities include:
Inventory and analysis of national cadastral datasets, primarily based on the Norwegian Cadastre (Matrikkelen) and supplementary geospatial datasets from:
•	The Norwegian Mapping Authority (Kartverket),
•	SSB’s building and dwelling statistics,
•	Land valuation data from the Norwegian Tax Administration,
•	Agricultural parcel registers (LREG, AR5),
•	Local zoning and land use plans (from municipalities or regional authorities).
Develop geospatial linking methodologies to connect cadastral parcels with statistical units such as buildings, addresses, dwellings, and socio-economic data at household level, with a strong emphasis on privacy and data security.

Enhance land and property statistics by generating derived indicators such as:
•	Parcel-based land use classification and change over time,
•	Land consumption per capita or household,
•	Parcel-level dwelling density and housing typology,
•	Distribution of public vs. private land ownership.

Demonstrate a cross-domain application, for instance:
Linking land parcels with agricultural statistics to improve land use efficiency indicators,
Integrating cadastral data with taxation and building data to inform spatial equity and housing affordability analysis.
Promote interoperability and reuse by adopting harmonized schemas aligned with INSPIRE and GSGF principles, including the use of standardized geocodes, unique identifiers, and open data models.
Document best practices and share results through Eurostat and EFGS platforms, including open-source tools, metadata, and technical documentation.

## Expected Outcomes:
•	A methodology for integrating cadastral and statistical data that can be adapted by other European NSIs.
•	Improved spatial granularity and thematic precision in official statistics related to land, property, housing, and land use.
•	New derived indicators that support national and EU land policies and SDG monitoring.
•	Clear documentation and technical guidance for interoperability, reproducibility, and sustainability.

## Contribution to Overall Project Goals
This WP aligns directly with the call’s objective to demonstrate the integration of geospatial and statistical data, focusing on one of the High-Value Dataset themes. It enhances collaboration between SSB and Kartverket (Norwegian Mapping Authority), contributes to reusable and open methodologies, and supports evidence-based policymaking across sectors.
